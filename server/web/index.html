Volatility trading: what if the stock moves more or less than priced?

The bank sells at the implied σ on the left and hedges with it, but the stock really moves at different realised vols. Calm stocks are cheap to hedge, wild ones expensive: the P&L depends on how much the stock moves, not which way.

Price a vanilla option by simulation

Simulates the stock's price at expiry many times in the risk-neutral world (it grows at the risk-free rate r, not its real expected return), averages the discounted payoff and reports the standard error (Hull 21.6). A vanilla option also has a Black-Scholes price, so this checks the engine before it prices options with no formula.

Convergence: more paths, narrower interval

The same option priced with more and more paths. The shaded band is the 95% interval; it narrows as 1/√N, so each extra decimal place of accuracy costs 100× the paths. That cost is why variance reduction (Hull 21.7) comes next.
